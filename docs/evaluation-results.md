## Reading the results

- **Constant ventilation is expensive and still not good enough.** Design
  flow day and night costs four times the energy of the other policies, and
  full lecture rooms still exceed 1000 ppm (70.7 % of their occupied samples
  within limits, peak 1088 ppm): at design flow a full room settles right
  around 1000 ppm, as the sizing check predicted.
- **Reactive control already does almost all of it.** It meets NFR-1 with two
  samples over 1000 ppm all week, at the lowest energy.
- **Anticipation is worth little in this building.** Even the oracle, with
  perfect knowledge of the future, only removes those two samples and lowers
  the peak from 1015 to 970 ppm, at about the same energy. The proposal (§7)
  said a small reactive-to-oracle gap would be a result worth reporting; here
  it is. The likely reasons: the damper can reach 1.5 × design flow within a
  few minutes, and lecture rooms (τ ≈ 12 min at design flow) catch up fast.
- **The predictor pays 10 % more energy than reactive for that small gain.** It
  ventilates for the 80 % quantile over the whole lead window, which
  deliberately over-forecasts.
- **The forecast warns ahead of fika breaks, but not of lectures.** In fika
  rooms it foresaw 72 % of arrivals (with false alarms in 17 % of quiet
  moments); "same as now" foresees none. In lecture rooms it foresaw none,
  because occupancysim puts each lecture in a random room every slot and day,
  so a room has no pattern to learn. A lecture timetable as an input (parked
  in architecture-options.md) is what would fix that. By mean absolute error
  the forecast is *worse* than "same as now" (fika 3.52 vs 1.95 people),
  because it always errs high by design; the error measure punishes that and
  ignores the warnings.
- **Comfort is weakest in lecture rooms under every policy** (84–88 % within
  20–24 °C). Not yet explained; see the open items.

## Caveats

- **2-minute resolution.** At factor 120 physics steps 2 model-minutes per
  real second, so there is one sample per room every ~2 model-minutes, not the
  60 s of FR-2. Shares below count samples. The training week (factor 300) has
  one occupancy reading per ~5 model-minutes, enough for 15-minute slots.
- **One gap.** The PC slept during `eval-constant`: no data Fri 25 Sep
  22:24–22:40 (empty building). Its energy is ~11 min of night flow short
  (~28 kWh), ~0.1 % of its total.
- **The building is a model we wrote.** The policies are compared against our
  own room physics; the result says as much about the model's time constants
  as about the controllers (proposal §8).
- **One seed, one week.** Day-to-day variation comes from occupancysim's
  generator; a second seed would show how stable the ranking is.
